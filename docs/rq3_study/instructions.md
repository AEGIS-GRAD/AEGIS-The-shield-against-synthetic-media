# Participant instructions

*Read aloud or hand to the participant before the first case. Wording matches the on-screen instructions.*

1. You will see eight short cases. In each one, an automated system has checked a piece of media (a video or a recording) to see whether it may be AI-generated or manipulated.
2. Read the result. Then say what you would do with the media, and answer three short statements about how you found the result.
3. Some results are clear. Others are uncertain or contradictory. “I can't decide without further checks” is a valid answer when you think more checking is needed.
4. All files, people and numbers in this study are invented. You are not being tested; we are testing how the results are presented.
5. Work at your own pace and go with your honest first impression. At the end there is a short questionnaire comparing the two ways results were shown to you.

A score is the system's estimate of how likely the media is to be AI-generated or manipulated: 0 means very unlikely, 1 means very likely.

## Researcher script (do not read the answers out)
- "There are no right or wrong impressions; I'm interested in how this looks to you."
- If asked what a term means in a case: "Use your own understanding; tell me what you took it to mean." (Note the term for the pilot findings.)
- Do not explain a result, hint at the expected answer, or react to answers.
- Optional think-aloud: "If you like, say what you're thinking as you read." (Pilot sessions: yes. Main study: only if all participants are asked.)
