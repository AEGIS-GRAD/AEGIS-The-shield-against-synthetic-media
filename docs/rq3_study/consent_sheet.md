## What is this study about?
AEGIS is a system that checks whether a video or recording may be AI-generated or manipulated. We want to know which way of showing its result helps people make good decisions and trust the result appropriately: a full evidence report, or a single score.

## What will I do?
You will look at eight short, invented cases on a screen. For each one you will say what you would do with the media and answer three short statements. At the end you will fill in a short questionnaire and choose which format you prefer. It takes about 25 to 30 minutes. A researcher is present and may take notes. Nothing is audio- or video-recorded unless you are told so and agree separately.

## Do I have to take part?
No. Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time, without giving a reason and without any consequence. If you stop, your answers are deleted if you ask.

## What data is collected?
- A participant code (for example P07). We do not collect your name on any answer sheet or in the software.
- Your answers, how long each case took, and two background questions (experience with checking media; use of detection tools).
- The researcher's notes on anything you found confusing.

## How is my data used and stored?
Data is stored under the participant code only, on the research team's password-protected storage. It is used for the project report and may be published only in anonymous, combined form. This signed sheet is kept separately from the answers, so your name cannot be linked to them.

## Risks and benefits
There are no known risks beyond everyday computer use. All media and people shown are invented. There is no payment. The benefit is helping improve how detection results are explained.
